AI accelerator hardware company
  • Designed and executed performance studies of Network-on-Chip (NoC) data movement in a distributed-memory AI accelerator, characterising throughput and scaling behaviour under unicast and multicast traffic patterns.
  • Built investigation infrastructure: contributed to the initial test suites used to instrument NoC traffic; reverse-engineered undocumented low-level NoC APIs to diagnose and fix improper usage in internal test suites; refactored shared visualisation tooling to generalise across all investigation types.
  • Characterised multicast write throughput across ten sender placement schemes on Wormhole and Blackhole, varying NoC selection and loopback; traced a >30% throughput degradation to write-acknowledge path contention on shared physical links — not virtual channel interference as initially suspected — and produced topology-specific placement guidelines (published in tt-low-level-documentation).
  • Demonstrated across one-to-one, one-to-all, and all-to-all traffic patterns that same-class VC parallelisation yields no throughput gain; attributed the null result to router arbitration ordering (VCs arbitrated before ports, wasting cycles on blocked VCs) and shared input buffer pressure as VC count increases — converted findings into architectural guidance on correct VC usage for traffic-class separation rather than bandwidth scaling.
