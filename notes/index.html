Control Theory — Classical

These notes cover ECE356 (Introduction to Control Theory) — the frequency-domain foundations of feedback systems: transfer functions, stability, Bode analysis, and root locus. Continued in ECE557 (state-space and modern methods) and BME331 (physiological control applications).

Second-Order Systems & Frequency Response

The second-order system is the canonical model in classical control. It captures the essential trade-off between speed and stability that appears everywhere — motor drives, analog filters, PLL loops, suspension systems. Its transfer function is parameterized by two quantities:

H(s)  =  ωn²  /  (s² + 2ζωns + ωn²)

ωn (natural frequency) sets the bandwidth — where on the frequency axis the system lives. ζ (damping ratio) governs the shape: how peaked the response is, where the poles sit in the s-plane, and whether the step response rings. The Bode plot is the frequency-domain fingerprint of this system.

Key observations

When ζ < 1/√2 ≈ 0.707, a resonant peak appears near ωn — the system amplifies inputs near its natural frequency. At ζ = 0.707 (maximally flat, Butterworth), the peak disappears and the −3 dB point is exactly ωn. Above ζ = 1 the system is overdamped — poles move onto the real axis and the response resembles two cascaded first-order filters. The high-frequency asymptote is always −40 dB/decade, regardless of damping.

Notice that sweeping ωn only translates the entire plot along the frequency axis — the shape is determined purely by ζ. This separation of concerns is what makes the normalized form of the transfer function so useful for design.
