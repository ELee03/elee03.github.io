Control Theory

Control theory is the study of how to make systems behave the way you want them to. That deceptively simple goal — from the thermostat to the spacecraft attitude controller — is grounded in a surprisingly unified mathematical framework. These notes trace it from classical frequency-domain intuition through modern state-space formalism.

Course Coverage
ECE356  ·  Introduction to Control Theory
Classical Frequency-Domain Methods
Transfer functions, block diagram algebra, steady-state error, Bode plots, gain and phase margin, root locus, and PID controller design. The core toolkit for practicing control engineers.
ECE557  ·  Linear Control Theory
State-Space & Modern Methods
State-space representations, solution of linear ODEs, stability via eigenvalues, controllability and observability, pole placement by state feedback, Luenberger observers, and an introduction to LQR optimal control.
BME331  ·  Physiological Control Systems
Biological Feedback & Control
Control-theoretic models of biological regulation: the pupillary light reflex, respiratory rhythm generation, cardiovascular control, and glucose-insulin dynamics. Connects the mathematics to real physiological systems.
